Who is really behind it?

The internet was made to connect people. Before you share what reaches you, bring it to us: a Bluesky, Mastodon or Telegram post, or an article from any news site. Within a minute you read who is behind it, whether a network of accounts is pushing it, what independent newspapers say, and how its words try to persuade you.

1. Who is behind it?
The account's age and reach, or the website's registration date, and whether either is on a public list: IFTAS's list of the Pravda network's accounts, or VIGINUM's and CheckFirst's lists of propaganda sites.
2. Is a network pushing it?
We read all of Bluesky live, plus the Telegram channels and Mastodon servers of the Pravda network. Is this post, its author, its link or its words part of a group acting together, a campaign or a contested story?
3. What do independent newspapers say?
NVIDIA Nemotron searches the news in the post's own country and language and in English, and counts distinct outlets in different countries. State media and listed sites never count as independent.
4. How does it try to persuade?
Fear appeals, loaded language, whataboutism, fabricated authority: each named with the exact words, translated if needed. Pictures and video are checked against the text.

We never accuse anyone: you get the evidence and how strong it is, and “not enough evidence” rather than a guess. A report stays in memory for six hours so you can share it, then it is forgotten; nothing is stored or posted. Pages behind a consent or login wall are not opened; we read the headline from the link instead.
